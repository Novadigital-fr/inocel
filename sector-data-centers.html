Carrying a Tier III facility through its grid works

(The situation)

Grid capacity arrives years after the demand

Connection queues are measured in years, and a data center cannot wait for its substation. The usual answer is a bank of diesel generators, which brings its own problems: combustion permits in dense urban areas, fuel deliveries through city traffic, exhaust stacks, and emissions that land squarely in scope 1 reporting.

A GEN-Z unit carries the same load with none of that. It emits water and heat, needs no combustion permit, and runs quietly enough to sit close to occupied buildings.

Deployed without touching the building

The unit arrives on a standard trailer and connects to your existing switchgear. There is no civil engineering, no fuel bund to build and no chimney to declare, which is what usually turns a six-week installation into a nine-month project.

For sites in dense urban areas, this is often the difference between a solution that can be permitted and one that cannot.

Monitored like the rest of your infrastructure

Every unit reports its state remotely, so servicing is scheduled on real operating data rather than on a fixed calendar. That is what lets us commit to availability figures rather than simply promise them.

The interface exposes the same signals your BMS already reads, so the unit does not become an island in your supervision.

Where operators put them

Bridging power

Carrying the site while the grid connection is being reinforced, for months or years at a time.

Zero-emission standby

Replacing diesel standby sets where local rules or corporate targets rule out on-site combustion.

Redundancy

Units running in parallel to reach N+1 without redesigning the electrical architecture.

Construction phase

Powering the build itself, before any permanent supply exists on the plot.

(Fuel supply)

The hydrogen is our responsibility, not yours

Certified low-carbon hydrogen is sourced, delivered and stored on your site by our teams. You sign one contract and call one number for the molecule and the machine.

Supply is where most hydrogen projects stall. Handling it ourselves is what makes the availability commitment possible in the first place.
